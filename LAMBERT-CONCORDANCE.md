is the **Read** item on each `Before Lecture x.y` frame and the module page:

almost entirely past this module — panel data, discrete choice, maximum

## What the title list shows about his method

Two things worth borrowing, neither of which needs a transcript:

**He splits the mathematics and the intuition into separate videos.** The
playlist carries "Estimated variance of OLS estimators — intuition behind
maths" as a companion to the derivation, and the same pattern for strict
exogeneity, instrumental variables, consistency and the Cramer-Rao bound. The
intuition is not a sentence inside the derivation; it is its own object with
its own title. That maps onto a choice this module already has to make on
every technical frame — and it argues for the intuition leading the frame and
the derivation sitting in the appendix, which is what we do.

**He teaches the moments as one family.** Separate short videos for moments,
central moments, skewness and kurtosis, adjacent in the playlist. That is
independent support for the frame added on 17 September that presents the three
as one construction at three powers, rather than as three definitions.

## Relevant but outside the module

Part 2 carries three Monte Carlo videos — an introduction, one for ordinary
least squares, and one for omitted variable bias in least squares. They are
not on this module's path, but they are the method this module is built on: a
known truth, repeated draws, and the estimate watched across them. A student
who wants to understand why we simulate rather than use real data would get
more from those three than from anything in part 1.

## Standing note

This concordance is an index of titles. The videos are Lambert's teaching and
stay his: they are linked and recommended, never transcribed into these decks
or used as source material for them. Where this module needs a citable
explanation, it uses the books on the shelf — Wooldridge for the notation and
the derivations, Angrist and Pischke for the framing.
